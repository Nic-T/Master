# Standards, Legislation, and Formats

## Electronic vs Digital Signatures

Understand the legal vs technical distinction:

- **Electronic Signature (legal concept):** Broadly, "data in electronic form which is attached to or logically associated with other data... and which is used by the signatory to sign." Examples: scanned handwritten signature images, voiceprints. Weakness: easily copied or tampered with.
- **Digital Signature (technical concept):** Subset of electronic signatures built on PKI; provides signer authenticity, integrity, and non-repudiation. Verifiable by third parties.

## Legal Framework — eIDAS

Regulation (EU) No 910/2014 defines signature levels:

- **Simple Electronic Signature:** Basic electronic data used to sign.
- **Advanced Electronic Signature (AdES):** Must meet Article 26 requirements:
  - Uniquely linked to the signatory
  - Capable of identifying the signatory
  - Created using data under the signatory's sole control
  - Linked to the signed data so that changes are detectable
- **Qualified Electronic Signature (QES):** An AdES created by a QSCD and based on a Qualified Certificate issued by a Qualified TSP; legally equivalent to a handwritten signature.

## Technical Standards (PKCS & ETSI)

Recognize common standards and formats:

- **PKCS**
  - PKCS#7 / CMS: Cryptographic Message Syntax — signing/encrypting messages (S/MIME).
  - PKCS#10: Certificate Signing Request (CSR) format.
  - PKCS#11: API for interacting with hardware tokens / HSMs.
  - PKCS#12: Container format for storing private keys and certificates (.p12 / .pfx).
- **ETSI AdES family**
  - CAdES: CMS-based advanced signatures (binary-oriented).
  - XAdES: XML-based advanced signatures (for structured data workflows).
  - PAdES: PDF-based advanced signatures (preserves visual representation).

## Long-Term Preservation (LTV)

Signatures and certificates age; LTV preserves trust over time:

- Problem: Certificates expire and algorithms may weaken; CRLs/OCSP responses may disappear.
- Solution: Timestamping (RFC 3161) and archival procedures (e.g., ETSI TS 119 511) — embed validation data, add periodic timestamps, and migrate to stronger algorithms when needed.

## Exam Gotchas

- **Signature vs Encryption:** Key Usage extension often separates signing from encryption; keys should not be reused across incompatible operations.
- **Sole Control:** For AdES/QES, the signer must maintain sole control over the private key (critical for remote signing architectures).
