# 4. Application layer security (Email)

## Overview ✅

Email security commonly relies on two standards: **PGP** and **S/MIME**. Both provide confidentiality and authentication but differ in trust model and key management.

---

## PGP (Pretty Good Privacy)

- **Trust model:** Web of Trust (decentralized) — users sign each other’s keys
- **Services:** Digital signatures (authentication), encryption (confidentiality), compression, Radix-64 encoding for email compatibility
- **Key management:** Users maintain a **public key ring** and **private key ring**
- Exam tip: PGP = peer-to-peer trust (signatures between users)

---

## S/MIME (Secure/Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions)

- **Trust model:** PKI (centralized) — relies on Certificate Authorities (CAs)
- **Message types:**
  - **EnvelopedData** — encrypted content (confidentiality)
  - **SignedData** — digital signature (authentication & integrity)
- **Encoding:** Base64 for binary-to-text in email
- Exam tip: S/MIME = X.509 certs issued by CAs

---

## Quick exam tips 💡

- PGP → **Web of Trust**; S/MIME → **PKI/CA**.
- Be prepared to identify which standard fits an example scenario (peer-signed key vs. CA-issued certificate).

---

## Abbreviations glossary 📖

- **CA** — Certificate Authority
- **PGP** — Pretty Good Privacy
- **PKI** — Public Key Infrastructure
- **S/MIME** — Secure/Multipurpose Internet Mail Extensions
